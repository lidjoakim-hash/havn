Department of Biosystems and Technology Third-cycle subject area: Agricultural Science PhD Project: Sustainability Assessment of Agroecological Farming Systems Description of the doctoral project The PhD project is a collaboration between the Animal Centred Environments group and the Horticultural and Agroecological Systems (HAS) subject area within the Department. The doctoral research will focus on sustainability assessment of agroecological livestock farming systems, drawing on expertise across both groups and involving collaboration with researchers, farmers and other stakeholders across Europe and Africa through ALASTRA and STRONG MCSA. About the position How can sustainability assessment support the transition to more resilient and sustainable farming systems? This PhD project will investigate how multi-criteria approaches can capture the environmental, economic and social performance of agroecological farming systems, identify synergies and trade-offs, and support decisions by farmers and other agricultural stakeholders. The doctoral student will work primarily within ALASTRA (Agroecology Living Labs in Africa: Redefining the Role of Animals in Food Systems for Climate Action and Sustainability), an EU-funded Horizon Europe project. ALASTRA brings together researchers, farmers and other stakeholders to co-design and evaluate agroecological livestock farming practices through living labs in Kenya, Tanzania, Tunisia and Zambia. SLU leads the development of the holistic assessment framework, including the selection of indicators and the design of sampling and data collection protocols. The doctoral student will contribute to developing, adapting and evaluating this framework. The research will draw on approaches such as the Global Farm Metric and established agroecological assessment tools, including OASISm the PG tool and TAPE. The doctoral student will also contribute to STRONG MCSA, an EU-funded Erasmus+ project that strengthens agricultural stakeholders’ capacity to use multi-criteria sustainability assessment (MCSA). These activities will provide a complementary foundation for the doctoral research in ALASTRA. The doctoral project will combine literature-based research, development and evaluation of assessment methods, quantitative and qualitative data analysis, and engagement with farmers, advisors and researchers. The student will collaborate with partners in Europe and Africa, contribute to project workshops and scientific publications, and communicate findings to academic and practitioner audiences. International visits and participation in project activities may form part of the doctoral studies.

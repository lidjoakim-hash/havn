Are you ready to take the next step in your academic journey? As a doctoral student with us, you will work closely with leading researchers in a strong, international research environment, with access to advanced laboratory equipment and solid funding opportunities. You will be offered secure employment, high-quality supervision, and room to grow, both as a researcher and as a person. At the Department of Computer Science, Electrical, and Space Engineering, we are looking for two Ph.D. students in Planetary Science. At LTU, Planetary Science is formally part of the research subject Atmospheric Science. Both projects investigate planetary solid surfaces. The projects will be conducted at Luleå University’s Space Campus in Kiruna.      Subject description    Atmospheric science deals with research on the atmosphere of Earth and other planets by modelling along with development and use of instruments for remote sensing and in situ observations. Special focus is on the atmosphere's dynamical, chemical, and physical processes, as well as better weather and climate predictions.      Project description    Both PhD projects address the evolution of volatile-rich surfaces on planetary bodies. You will be welcomed into a supportive group of researchers in Luleå University of Technology’s Division of Space Technology, whose focus is on investigating physical properties and processes of solar system bodies using the laboratory experiments and spacecraft measurements.      Project A addresses an enigmatic process without terrestrial equivalent: the formation of crusts on the martian surface. You will be tasked with reproducing a crust-like surface regolith in the laboratory under Martian conditions, measure its thermal and mechanical properties, attempt to derive mechanical properties from imagery and accelerometer data of past missions to Mars including InSight and rovers, and compare these. Your results will inform drilling and sample return missions to Mars and your findings may help mitigate the risk to ExoMars and other planetary exploration missions of the future and support projects on planetary mobility in science, education and engineering.      Project B is, quite literally, even cooler: it will investigate the surfaces of the icy moons in the outer Solar System. The project will simulate volatile extrusion and ejection, precipitation, crust formation and surface evolution processes in cold vacuum environments. Using laboratory simulations and physical models, you will investigate the link between volatile deposition, surface morphology, and surface mechanical properties. You will compare your results to theoretical models and spacecraft imagery. Your results will help explain spacecraft observations and inform future space missions to the outer solar system. As in project A, the outcomes of this project are expected to be useful for future missions aiming to explore the surfaces of minor bodies in the outer solar system, such as Ceres, Enceladus and Europa.     Duties    As a PhD student you are expected to perform both experimental and theoretical work within your research studies. You will communicate your results at national and international conferences and in scientific journals. Most of your working time will be devoted to your own research project, of which you are gradually expected to take ownership.    In collaboration with your supervisors, you will engage in the following research activities:   -laboratory experiments involving environmental simulations;   -analytical modelling of experimental results;   -analysis of remote sensing data and comparison with experimental results;   -dissemination of results at international conferences and in scientific publications.      In addition to learning and improving your skills while performing these tasks, you will conduct literature reviews and attend courses as part of your graduate education.

Western Norway University of Applied Sciences (HVL) has a vacancy for a Postdoctoral Fellow in restoration ecology for a 3 year period About the position: We are looking for a highly motivated and talented postdoctoral fellow to join the Landscape, Ecology and Climate (LEC) research group at the Department of Civil Engineering and Environmental Sciences, at the Sogndal campus. The position is funded through the national, transdisciplinary research centre Landfutures, as part of its research area &#34;Knowledge and worldviews&#34;. The Post Doc is a temporary, three-year position, but may be extended to four years with an additional year of teaching duties at the department.  If you are interested in supporting sustainable land use by developing better prediction methods in restoration using advanced statistical modelling, and combine it with field work, this vacancy could be for you.  The place of work for the position is campus Sogndal.  Startingdate 01.05.2027 Key duties and responsibilities: About Landfutures Landfutures is an eight-year research centre for sustainable land and nature use (2026–2034), funded by the Research Council of Norway. The centre is led by Western Norway Research Institute and run jointly by Western Norway University of Applied Sciences, the Norwegian Mapping Authority, the Institute of Transport Economics, Nordland Research Institute, Ruralis Institute for Rural and Regional Research, the Norwegian Central Computing Centre and the Basque Centre for Climate Change in Spain. Landfutures aims to promote sustainable land use through transformative research and practice and intends to be a hub for research into profound societal changes in the way we use nature and land. Background and research need Sustainable land use acknowledges ecosystem restoration as a crucial strategy for halting biodiversity loss. Today, significant resources are invested in ecosystem restoration, yet our understanding of the long-term outcomes of restoration measures remains limited, partly due to insufficient monitoring and evaluation.   The Postdoctoral Fellow will explore methods for assessing restoration success of degraded ecosystems and supplement existing data from previous, long-term restoration studies with extra vegetation surveys. Emphasis will be placed on development of the ORBA (ordination regression-based analysis) approach. Concrete recommendations for how restoration projects can be monitored and evaluated shall be shared with practitioners. Ultimately, the work of the postdoctoral fellow will underpin evidence-based ecosystem restoration and contribute to Landfutures’ aim of strengthening sustainable land use and support well-functioning ecosystems. Research environment The postdoc fellow will be part of the LEC research group, an interdisciplinary group of ecologists and social scientists. We offer a stimulating, interdisciplinary academic environment. The postdoctoral  fellow will have their place of work and affiliation at campus Sogndal, and  work closely with the staff members of LEC, particularly Inger Auestad and Knut Rydgren. The postdoctoral fellow will receive an annual stipend for conference participation, research visits, and equipment. Through the transdisciplinary Landfutures centre, the postdoc will benefit from working with experts at the intersection of ecology, planning, political science, social anthropology and geography and in interaction with other Post Doc Fellows, PhD candidates and master’s students, as well as with relevant stakeholders.  Qualifications: Criteria for appointment to the position of postdoctoral fellow are regulated in Forskrift til universitets- og høyskoleloven. Applicants must have earned a PhD in restoration ecology, vegetation ecology, or in a closely related field. Experience from botanical field work in Norwegian boreal ecosystems, and strong competence in floristics, including cryptogams, is mandatory. The candidate must have strong competence in (multivariate) statistical analyses and statistical modelling, particularly generalized linear models. A driver’s license is desirable. The applicant must have completed the PhD or have received confirmation of the date of defence before the application deadline Applicants will be assessed primarily on the basis of: • Relevant research experience and high-quality publications on restoration ecology  • Competence and grades on completed course work • Relevant experience in vegetation fieldwork in Norway or elsewhere in Scandinavia is an advantage. • Previous involvement in relevant research projects • A strong command of English and Norwegian or another Scandinavian language, both orally and in writing Preference will be given to applicants with: • Strong, practical programming skills in R or another programming language for data analysis • Previous experience in supporting relevant MSc and BSc projects • Previous experience in teaching relevant subjects The following applicant characteristics will also be considered: • Confidence in working independently as a researcher, and in cross-disciplinary teams  • Ability to lead the writing of research papers, to initiate and advance research, and strong research and scientific leadership potential • Potential for establishing relevant national and international research contacts • Excellent communication and collaboration skills • Capacity for dissemination and ability to positively contribute to advancement of nature restoration research at HVL Applicants should explicitly address the qualifications and ranking criteria listed above in their application and provide sufficient documentation in their application to support the listed qualifications.

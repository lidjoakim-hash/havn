We are seeking a postdoctoral fellow to join our group to explore the mechanisms of microbiota-host mucin interactions Subject area  Glycobiology Subject area description  The human gut microbiota colonizes the colonic mucus layer that acts as a protective barrier between bacteria and the intestinal epithelium. The major component of the mucus layer are mucins, glycoproteins decorated with complex O-glycans. Some members of the human microbiota can bind to and utilize these complex glycans. We aim to identify how different gut bacteria utilize complex host glycans. Duties  In this project, the candidate will study how gut bacteria evolved to recognize host glycans. The candidate will use proteomics to identify putative carbohydrate-active enzymes from gut bacteria species that are implicated in host glycan utilization. The identified enzymes will be recombinantly expressed and characterized using a combination of biochemical assays. Additionally, the candidate will generate deletion mutants of putative key enzymes to determine their impact on glycans utilization. The aim of this project is to identify key enzymatic steps required for the degradation of host glycans by the gut microbiota. Those who have completed their degree more than three years prior to the end of the application period may also be considered in the first instance if special grounds exist. Special grounds relate to leave of absence due to illness, parental leave, commissions of trust within union organisations, service within the defence services or other similar circumstances, as well as clinical service or service/assignment relevant to the subject area.   Assessment criteria  The ideal candidate will have documented experience in anaerobic microbiology, proteomics and biochemical techniques such as cloning, recombinant expression of proteins and characterization of carbohydrate-active enzymes. Experience in mucin biology, host glycans and bioinformatics will be considered as merits in support of an application. Previous experience in analysis of complex glycans by mass spectrometry and in structural biology is also a merit.

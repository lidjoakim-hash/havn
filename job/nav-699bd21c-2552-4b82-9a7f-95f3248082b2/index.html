About the position A full-time researcher position (SKO 1109) is available at the Center for Lifespan Changes in Brain and Cognition (LCBC), Department of Psychology (PSI), Faculty of Social Sciences, University of Oslo. The researcher will work full time on the Horizon Europe project SHIELD - Sources of Health Inequalities from Environmental Pollution in Lifespan Brain Development, funded by the Horizon Europe programme. SHIELD investigates how environmental exposures affect brain health and brain diseases across the entire lifespan, from conception to old age.  The position will focus on environmental exposure modelling and the generation of environmental exposure maps for the SHIELD cohorts, as well as MRI data analysis. We are particularly looking for a candidate who can work at the interface of brain imaging and environmental exposure data, with a strong research background in MRI data analysis and experience with using environmental and geographic databases. Transdisciplinary experience and interest will be an advantage, as the consortium involves partners from multiple fields, including (but not limited to) psychology, medicine, physics, environmental epidemiology and statistical modelling.  As SHIELD is expected to generate significant impacts, the researcher will be expected also to contribute to stakeholder engagement and science communication activities, for example by participating in stakeholder workshops and contributing to policy briefs, newsletters, and social media posts.  The research position is funded until the end of the project, 31 December 2031. LCBC is the coordinator of the SHIELD consortium, which includes 13 partners, and this position will be one of the key roles in the entire consortium. The position and its associated tasks are funded only for the stated period and will end when the funding period expires. More about the position and the research center The goal of LCBC is to understand differences and changes in brain and cognition across the entire lifespan, and to identify how brain function and cognition can be optimized. LCBC is an active, multidisciplinary research center with around 25 full-time staff members, including four professors, two researchers, several postdoctoral fellows and PhD candidates, chief and senior data engineers, full- and part-time research assistants, administrative support, and several affiliated international researchers.  LCBC has excellent research infrastructure, including access to a 3T Siemens Prisma MRI scanner, PET and EEG facilities, and comprehensive neuropsychological and somatic testing facilities. English is the main working language at the center. LCBC has secured several large grants, including four European Research Council (ERC) grants, a Horizon 2020 grant, and several grants from the Research Council of Norway, the National Health Association of Norway, and the University of Oslo. In 2015, the group was designated “world-leading” and is one of only five such groups at the University of Oslo. More information is available on the LCBC website: https://www.lcbc.uio.no.  Your main tasks will be • Processing and analyzing multimodal brain MRI (structural, diffusion, and quantitative imaging) and cognitive trajectory and modeling these in relation to exposures.  • Processing and analyzing behavioral and health data and modeling these in relation to brain and exposure data.  • Characterizing and modelling historical air pollution concentrations (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, etc.) for the SHIELD cohorts by integrating Chemical Transport Model (CTM) simulation outputs with emission inventories and observational datasets.  • Generating environmental exposure maps extending back to 1920 by combining diverse open-source datasets (meteorological, emissions, and land-use data) for SHIELD's Age-Period-Cohort (APC)-corrected analyses. • Contribute to harmonization, expansion and application of EXPANSE maps.  • Collaborate with other SHIELD researchers and participate in the SHIELD consortium meetings.  • Write and publish scientific articles.  • Contribute to stakeholder engagement and science communication activities of SHIELD: participating at workshops, writing policy-briefs, newsletters, social media posts etc. Qualifications In this role you must have  • PhD in a relevant field such as Neuroscience, Meteorology, Physics, Biophysics, Complex systems, Data Modelling, or a highly related, quantitative natural science/interdisciplinary field.  • Research experience in Computational Neuroimaging analysis (structural, functional, diffusion, or quantitative MRI).  • Experience with the use of meteorological/climate, spatial, or similar multidimensional databases.  • Strong programming skills in Python and/or R.  • Very good proficiency in written and spoken English is a prerequisite. It is an advantage if you have • Experience with secure computing and data-handling environments suitable for sensitive human cohort data.  • Experience with research in interdisciplinary/transdisciplinary research environments.  • Experience in science communication and outreach activities.

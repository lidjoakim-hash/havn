About the project We are looking for a PhD candidate to be part of an interdisciplinary team investigating geographic variation in how European insects cope with the combined stress of higher temperatures and drier conditions. There is growing evidence that insect populations are declining around the world, making the proposed research timely and important. This project builds on research comparing the physiological responses of insect species from contrasting climates to better understand future responses to changes in temperature and precipitation. The PhD position will be focused primarily on understanding the plastic and evolved responses of insects to temperature and hydric stress. The expected research will include a combination of field and lab work, including insect husbandry, and physiological experiments characterizing thermal and desiccation tolerance. The position will require statistical analyses and potentially the development of eco-evolutionary biophysical models. The results of this work are expected to be disseminated in the scientific literature as well as to the public and relevant stakeholders. The primary supervisor for this position will be Professor Cameron Ghalambor. Duties of the position • Complete the doctoral education until obtaining a doctorate • Carry out research of high quality within the framework described above • Academic publications and popular science dissemination • Participate in the Biodiversity, Ecology, and Evolution research group as part of the Ghalambor lab  • Participate in international activities such as conferences and/or research stays at foreign educational institutions • Teaching experience in courses related to Ecology and Evolution Be prepared for changes to your work duties after employment. Required selection criteria • You must meet the requirements for admission to the PhD programme in Biology, see Section 6-1 of the PhD regulations for more information. • You must have a relevant Master's degree in biology or equivalent with relevant background in evolutionary biology, ecology, entomology, physiology, or similar. Your degree must include a major independent project (minimum 30 ECTS) equivalent to a master’s thesis • Master students can apply, but the master's degree must be obtained and documented before starting the position and no later than December 2026. • You must have a strong and relevant academic background from your previous studies and have an average grade from your Master's degree study, or equivalent education, which is equal to B or better compared to NTNU's grading scale. If you do not have letter grades from previous studies, you must have an equally good academic foundation. If you have a weaker academic background, you may be considered if you can document that you are exceptionally suited for a PhD education, for example through relevant work experience and/or peer-reviewed academic works. • You have excellent written and oral English language skills  • You have some experience conducting statistical analyses using R or other statistical languages. The appointment is to be made in accordance with NTNUs guidelines for recruitment positions for general criteria for the position.

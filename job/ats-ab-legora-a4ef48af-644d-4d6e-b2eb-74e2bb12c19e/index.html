We work across three streams in parallel: building internal agents and tools, equipping teams to build their own, and owning the standards, access and guardrails that let everyone move fast safely. We own the AI platforms and the internal products built on them. So far that includes Leya, an internal knowledge agent live in Slack that answers questions about Legora with cited sources and automates parts of our RFP and security questionnaire work. A structured, agent-readable knowledge base assembled from knowledge each domain continues to own, where agents propose changes and humans approve them. Leyf, a platform for internally built apps that lands anyone's app on shared infrastructure with guardrails scaled to how much damage it could do. And a Skills Hub, so that something one person works out becomes something the whole company can use rather than staying on their laptop. The team is small and the mandate is large. That is the job. WHAT MAKES THIS HARD Legora is an AI company of around a thousand people, most already fluent and on current tools. Internal AI here is not an adoption problem. Nobody needs convincing, which means there is nowhere to hide behind change management or training. If something we build is not used, it is because it was not good enough. Our colleagues know what good looks like and will not use something worse than what they think they could build themselves in an afternoon. So we ship things people genuinely want, and the foundations arrive with them. That constraint does not go away. The mandate is also larger than the team. The part no individual can do for themselves is the infrastructure, the access model and the architecture underneath, and getting that right across a company this size is the actual work. It does not come with a specification.

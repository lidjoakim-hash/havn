As a PhD Candidate with us, you will work to achieve your doctorate and, at the same time, gain valuable experience that qualifies you for a further career in higher education and research, in and outside academia. The Department of Electric Energy (IEL) at NTNU is seeking a highly motivated candidate for a full-time (100%) three-year PhD position in Power System State Estimation. The PhD candidate will contribute to research activities within two ongoing research projects, Power Stability Wide Area Monitoring Protection (p-SWAMP) and Innovative Technologies for Massive Renewables Integration into the Grid (INNO4GRID), and will work closely with researchers involved in these projects.  The research is expected to focus on advanced methods for power system state estimation, with particular emphasis on bad data detection and potential applications in voltage stability assessment. The exact research scope may be further refined based on the candidate’s background and research interests, while remaining within the broader field of power system state estimation. You will join the research group Power System Operation and Analysis (PSOA) at IEL, where we foster an open, inclusive, and collaborative working environment. Our work environment is characterized by a friendly and supportive atmosphere, with regular activities such as research group meetings, weekly colloquia, shared lunches, and “Friday coffee” sessions. These formal and informal gatherings provide opportunities to exchange ideas, celebrate milestones, and build strong professional and social connections. PhD candidates also organize social activities open to everyone interested, contributing to a welcoming and inclusive community. Trondheim offers a vibrant student environment combined with easy access to nature, providing both an active social setting and quiet surroundings for focused research. Your immediate leader will be the Head of Department. About the project Power system state estimation is a key function in transmission system operation, as many higher-level monitoring, security assessment, and control applications rely on reliable estimates of the system state. State estimators combine measurements with mathematical grid models and must remain robust to erroneous data and missing measurements. Building on these challenges, the PhD candidate will contribute to research activities within the p-SWAMP project. The research focuses on the development and evaluation of linear state estimation methods under realistic operating conditions, including challenges related to power system observability. Furthermore, the research investigates how uncertainties in state estimation affect the reliability of voltage stability indicators. The main supervisor for the PhD is Associate Professor Daniel Baltensperger, and the co-supervisor will be Professor Kjetil Obstfelder Uhlen.

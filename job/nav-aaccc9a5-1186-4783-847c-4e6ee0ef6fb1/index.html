The position The Department of Physics and Technology is thrilled to announce a vacant position of Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the area of Experimental Cancer Biophysics and Quantitative Bioimaging at UiT The Arctic University of Norway, Faculty of Science and Technology. The position is connected to the project Cymoplive funded by UiT.  The position is a fixed term position for a duration of 3 years. It is a prerequisite that the applicant can carry out the project over the full course of the employment period. No person may hold more than one fixed term position as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the same institution. As a Postdoctoral Research Fellow, you will have the opportunity to advance your scientific career and further qualify for academic positions. If you do not already have the educational competence that meets the requirements for an Associate Professor position in Norway, UiT will arrange such competence during the employment period. The workplace is at UiT in Tromsø. You must be able to start in the position within a reasonable time after receiving the offer. The position's field of research The project focuses on the development and validation of Cymoplive, a tumor-on-chip functional assay platform that uses longitudinal, label-free microscopy to investigate cancer cell responses and adaptation to therapeutic interventions. The central research objective is to identify and establish novel imaging-based functional markers of therapeutic response by investigating changes in cancer cell behaviour at both single-cell and population levels. The research will integrate advanced live-cell label-free microscopy, quantitative image analysis, and experimentally controlled therapeutic perturbations to characterize cellular motility, morphological dynamics, phenotypic heterogeneity, and adaptive responses. Using established and patient-derived cancer models, the project will investigate how these behaviours evolve under different therapeutic agents, dosing strategies, and treatment schedules. The identified markers will be quantitatively characterized and validated for their sensitivity, reproducibility, and biological relevance. Their performance will be benchmarked against conventional drug-response and complementary imaging-based assays, while their relationships with molecular markers of cancer cell plasticity will be investigated. The overarching goal is to establish robust imaging-based measures that provide additional insight into therapeutic responses beyond conventional assay endpoints, supporting their application in preclinical drug development. The position offers opportunities to develop independent research questions and methodological approaches within the project’s broader scientific objectives. Key Responsibilities: • Development and optimization of quantitative microscopy and longitudinal live-cell imaging methods.   • Design and execution of drug-response experiments using cancer cell models and tumor-on-chip platforms, including clinically relevant treatment regimens.   • Development and application of conventional and AI-based image-processing and quantitative analysis methods. • Development and validation of quantitative metrics to characterize cancer cell adaptation, functional heterogeneity, and drug responses. • Correlation of functional responses with molecular markers of cancer cell plasticity using ICC, IF, and related techniques. • Comparative evaluation of therapeutic responses across different cancer models and treatments. • Benchmarking against conventional viability assays and complementary imaging-based and functional assays. • Statistical analysis, assessment of measurement reproducibility, and contribution to scientific publications and collaborative research.

About SFI FAST - Future Aluminium Structures SFI FAST (Future Aluminium Structures) is a national Centre for Research-based Innovation (SFI) funded by the Research Council of Norway and the aluminum industry. The centre is led by NTNU in close collaboration with SINTEF and a broad consortium of 16 leading industrial partners across the aluminium value chain. FAST runs from 2026 to 2033 and represents one of Norway’s most ambitious long-term research initiatives within aluminium materials, manufacturing technologies, and circular economy. The primary objective of FAST is to establish the scientific and technological knowledge base required to enable the large-scale use of postconsumer scrap (PCS) Aluminium in high-value, structural, and safety-critical products. FAST adopts a through-scale and through-process research methodology, integrating materials science, manufacturing processes, product and structural design, and lifecycle performance. A key outcome of FAST is the development of the FAST Virtual Lab, a digital framework combining experimental data, physics-based models, and data-driven methods to support design, manufacturing, and decision-making across Aluminium value chains. Education and competence building are central pillars of FAST. The centre will educate more than 18 PhD and postdoctoral researchers and over 100 MSc students, strengthening Norway’s international position in Aluminium research and innovation.  Please see the centre’s website: Future Aluminium Structures (FAST) - NTNU Duties of the position This postdoctoral position aims to address the following topics as critical steps toward the reliable fabrication of recycled aluminum structures through sustainable, data-driven welding routes. Therefore, the main tasks of the position are as follows: • Process Parameter Development: Development and implementation of optimal arc and solid-state welding characteristics  • Monitoring & Control: Developing an in-situ monitoring and control system to detect, characterize, and correct/mitigate defects during welding • Characterization: Evaluating the microstructure and mechanical performance of fabricated aluminum components • Process–performance correlation and data-assisted analysis: Establishing experimentally grounded relationships between real-time process monitoring data, microstructure, and mechanical performance to support process optimization and reliability assessment. • Proposal development: Development of nationally (NFR) and internationally (EU) funded proposals, particularly with close collaboration with industry. • Collaboration of PhD and M.Sc. projects: You will have a central role in connecting WP4 and WP5 in SFI FAST, where two PhD students will work on Welding Process Development and Fatigue Performance and Enhancement • Dissemination: Presentation of research outcomes in the SFI FAST Center and publishing the project outcomes in high-quality journals  • Contribute to project meetings, workshops, seminars etc. You will report to Prof. Hosseini and Assoc. Professor Nima Razavi.

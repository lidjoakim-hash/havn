About the position We invite applications for a three-year PhD Research Fellow position in experimental proton therapy, available at the Department of Physics, University of Oslo (UiO). The preferred starting date is March 1, 2027, and no later than April 1, 2027. The fellowship period is 36 months. The position is part of InnovaRTBRAIN – Innovative Radiation Therapy for Brain Tumors, a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Network (MSCA-DN) funded by the European Union. The network brings together leading European research institutions, hospitals, and industrial partners to develop innovative radiation therapy approaches for brain tumors, with particular emphasis on glioblastoma. The successful candidate will be recruited as Doctoral Candidate 13 (DC13) and will join the proton therapy research group at the Department of Physics, University of Oslo and the Department of Radiation Biology, Oslo University Hospital (OUS). Most of the experimental research will be carried out at the proton therapy facility at Oslo University Hospital, which provides state-of-the-art infrastructure for preclinical proton irradiation, including a dedicated research beamline and gantry, a 3T small-animal MRI scanner, and facilities for experimental radiobiology. No one can be appointed for more than one PhD Research Fellowship period at the University of Oslo.  Background of the project Glioblastoma is one of the most aggressive forms of brain cancer, with limited treatment options and poor prognosis. Radiation therapy plays a central role in its treatment, but its effectiveness is restricted by tumor radioresistance and the sensitivity of surrounding healthy brain tissue. Proton minibeam radiation therapy (pMBRT) is an emerging approach in which radiation is delivered in narrow, spatially separated beams, producing a highly heterogeneous dose distribution. This technique has the potential to reduce radiation-induced damage to healthy tissues while maintaining effective tumor irradiation. However, its biological effects, particularly the evolution of tumor and normal tissue responses over time, remain incompletely understood.  The PhD project will investigate the biological response to proton minibeam irradiation in preclinical models of brain cancer, using longitudinal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and quantitative image analysis. The project will also explore the potential of MRI-derived biomarkers for assessing treatment response and normal tissue effects. An important part of the research will be conducted in close collaboration with another doctoral candidate in InnovaRTBRAIN (DC6), based at Oslo University Hospital, who will investigate combinations of radiation therapy and immunotherapy, including CAR-T cell treatment. Job description  The doctoral candidate will conduct experimental studies in mouse models to investigate tumor and normal tissue responses to proton minibeam radiation therapy, using magnetic resonance imaging and other methods to characterize radiation-induced biological effects. The main responsibilities will be: • Plan and conduct preclinical proton minibeam irradiation experiments in mouse models • Acquire and analyze MRI data, including diffusion-weighted imaging, to quantify treatment response and potential normal tissue toxicity • Relate MR-derived image parameters to radiation dose distributions, linear energy transfer (LET), and biological endpoints • Develop and apply quantitative image analysis and radiomics methods to identify imaging features associated with tumor response and radiation injury • Investigate MRI-based biomarkers in combination with NMR-derived metabolic and relaxation-time markers, in collaboration with international project partners • Collaborate with DC6 on preclinical studies combining proton minibeam radiation therapy and immunotherapy • Participate in joint research, training activities, and research visits within the InnovaRTBRAIN doctoral network Research environment The candidate will join an interdisciplinary research environment combining radiobiology, medical physics, experimental proton therapy, and medical imaging. The research will be conducted in close collaboration between the University of Oslo and Oslo University Hospital, primarily at the proton therapy research facility at the Norwegian Radium Hospital. The facility provides access to experimental proton irradiation, a dedicated small-animal MRI scanner, treatment planning software, and infrastructure for preclinical cancer research. Supervisors and international collaboration The doctoral candidate will be supervised by Professor Eirik Malinen (UiO/OUS), with co-supervision from Professor Nina Edin (UiO), Professor Paul Vasos (University of Bucharest), and Else Marit Inderberg (OUS). The InnovaRTBRAIN network will provide an international and interdisciplinary training environment, combining expertise in medical physics, radiobiology, imaging, cancer biology, and immunotherapy. Planned secondments As part of the InnovaRTBRAIN doctoral network, the candidate will undertake research visits to international academic and industrial partners: • University of Bucharest, Romania (3 months): Integration of NMR-based biomarkers with MRI-derived radiomics to characterize tumor responses. • Xstrahl, UK (2 weeks): Training in small-animal irradiation technologies and their applications in preclinical radiation therapy research. • COSYLAB, Slovenia (2 weeks): Exposure to industrial developments in radiation therapy technology and control systems. These secondments will provide complementary scientific and technical training and strengthen collaboration within the InnovaRTBRAIN network. What skills are important in this role?

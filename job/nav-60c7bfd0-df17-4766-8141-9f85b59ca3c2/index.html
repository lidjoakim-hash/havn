You will find more information about working at NTNU and the application process here.    Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v&#61;_KHQjc4ndas&t&#61;41sAbout the position The PhD position is part of the Norwegian Maritime AI Centre, led by NTNU. The centre brings together researchers, industry and public partners to develop AI applications for the maritime sector.  The PhD project will develop AI methods for navigational situational awareness and risk-based route planning, with applications in safe navigation and wind-assisted propulsion. A key question is how maritime data can be used to assess navigational risk and inform route and speed choices, accounting for vessel capabilities and uncertainty in the available information.  The work will draw on two of the center's use cases: AI-driven S-100 electronic navigational chart products (UC3) and wind-assisted propulsion (UC5). UC3 concerns the use of electronic navigational chart data to assess hazards along a vessel’s path. The use case will investigate how these assessments can account for the vessel’s position and motion, together with characteristics such as draught, stopping distance and maneuvering capabilities. Historical AIS tracks combined with chart data offer a basis for constructing and studying navigational situations. It is also a goal of the use case that these assessments should be explainable. That is, it should be possible to trace a warning or recommendation to the data and criteria that produced it. In UC5, the focus is on route and speed planning for vessels with wind-assisted propulsion. Taking advantage of wind conditions can reduce fuel consumption, but the benefits depend on the vessel’s performance and the route it can safely follow. The use case will investigate both how forecasts of wind and other environmental conditions can be used in voyage planning, accounting for navigational and operational constraint, and how adaptations can be made underway, accounting for actual conditions.  The two use cases link the assessment of individual navigational situations to planning over a longer voyage. The project will examine how uncertainty in data and forecasts affects these decisions, and how plans can be revised as conditions change. Method development will build on machine learning in combination with established models of vessel behavior and navigation. The choice of methods will be developed as part of the doctoral work. The candidate will work closely with industry and public partners in the centre. The PhD candidate will be supervised by Associate Professor Børge Rokseth. About the project DThe Norwegian Maritime AI Centre shall position the maritime sector for accelerated and successful use of AI. The demand for safe and sustainable maritime activities, as well as the shortage of crew and engineers, are key drivers for change in the maritime sector. At the same time, there is a pull for growth, increased efficiency and profitability, while managing volatility in the markets. Moreover, the sector is critically important for global supply chains and security in a tense geopolitical situation. To demonstrate and prototype maritime AI solutions, the center will address a diverse and impactful set of use cases: Logistics, shipping and business transactions; Ship design and construction; Voyage planning and optimization; Condition monitoring and maintenance; Crew training; Maritime traffic and ship surveillance; Decarbonization and energy management; Autonomous ships and remote control.  The research partners are the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), University of Oslo, Oslo School of Architecture and Design, Royal Norwegian Naval Academy, Norwegian Mapping Authority, MET and SINTEF Nordvest. There are about 30 user partners from the maritime sector, in addition to hundreds of members of the Maritime Forum, Blue Maritime, Ocean Autonomy, and Maritime CleanTech clusters. The center will be a hub, orchestrating the interfaces with research partners and user partners to facilitate collaboration, knowledge creation, dissemination, innovation, and sharing of knowledge. The Norwegian Maritime AI Center is funded by the Research Council of Norway and the partners for the period 2026-2030. There will be approximately 25 doctoral scholarships and some postdoc positions.

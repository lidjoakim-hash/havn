You will find more information about working at NTNU and the application process here.    Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v&#61;_KHQjc4ndas&t&#61;41sAbout the position The Department of ICT and Natural Sciences (IIR) at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) in Ålesund is opening a PhD position in distributed informative planning for teams of autonomous and remotely operated marine vehicles, with applications in coastal environmental monitoring. Coastal waters and fjords change continuously. Pollutants spread, floating litter drifts with wind and currents, and water quality and biological activity vary across both space and time. A single vehicle can only observe a small part of such an environment at once. Teams of unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), supported by unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), can cover larger areas, observe processes from several viewpoints at the same time, and respond to what they detect. To use such teams effectively, the vehicles must build a shared and predictive picture of the environment and decide together where each of them should go next. They must do this under the real constraints of the marine environment, including limited communication, changing weather and heterogeneous vehicles. The position is part of NTNU Ålesund’s work on ocean intelligence: collecting data about the ocean to support use cases such as monitoring of fish spawning grounds, mapping of microplastics and suspended contamination and water quality monitoring.  The position builds on ongoing research at the department on autonomous surface vehicles, informative path planning, online mapping of spatial fields and tracking of drifting targets, which has been validated in field trials. The PhD project will extend these methods from single vehicles to cooperating teams of heterogeneous vehicles. The research will combine theory, simulation and field experiments. Field work will be carried out in coastal waters near Ålesund with the department's fleet of USVs and UAVs. The successful candidate will contribute to one or more of the following areas: • Multi-robot coordination for monitoring spatially and temporally varying phenomena, such as drifting objects, pollutant plumes and evolving environmental fields, including distributed informative path and trajectory planning. • Mobile robot sensing, mapping and active decision making for heterogeneous teams of USVs and UAVs, using methods such as model predictive control, sampling-based planning, probabilistic mapping and machine learning, under limited and intermittent communication. • Implementation on real vehicles and validation in field experiments, including remote operation and supervision of the team Within the scope of the project, the PhD research can be adapted to the candidate's interests and background, with emphasis on planning and control, estimation and mapping, or learning-based methods. Your immediate leader will be the Head of Department.

About the position At the Centre for Precision Psychiatry, Institute of Clinical Medicine, University of Oslo, there is a full-time, 2,5 years position available as a Researcher in artificial intelligence (AI), statistical modelling, and computational method development. We invite applications from highly motivated candidates with a strong background in mathematics, statistics, computer science, data science, artificial intelligence, machine learning, computational modelling, engineering, physics, bioinformatics, or a related quantitative discipline. The position is part of an international and interdisciplinary research environment at the forefront of AI-driven precision neuroscience and precision medicine. The position is part of a collaborative effort between the University of Oslo, Oslo University Hospital, UC San Diego, the J. Craig Venter Institute (JCVI), and affiliated partners. The successful candidate will contribute to the development, implementation, and application of computational and AI-based methods for integrating multimodal brain imaging, genomics, registry, clinical, and other biomedical data, with the aim of improving understanding, prediction, and risk stratification of dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, and related neuropsychiatric disorders. The position is linked to the “ResiliDem” project and the “Talent Utland” programme, funded by the Research Council of Norway. This is a unique opportunity to work on next-generation AI and statistical modelling approaches for large-scale, longitudinal, and potentially federated datasets, bridging computational innovation, biology, and clinical applications. More about the position The Centre for Precision Psychiatry is dedicated to research on severe mental and neuropsychiatric disorders. The Centre runs multidisciplinary studies involving hospital departments in Oslo, research groups at the University of Oslo, and international collaborators. The main goal is to develop tools and knowledge for precision medicine in brain disorders, using advanced statistical methods and AI tools for the analysis of large-scale genetic, neuroimaging, registry, clinical, and molecular data. The Centre has access to large datasets including biomarkers, clinical and cognitive variables, genotyping and sequencing data, and MRI brain imaging data. In addition, we work closely with national population cohorts such as Tromsø, HUSK, and HUNT, Nordic registry and biobank resources, and large international consortia including PGC, ENIGMA, ADNI, NBDC, UK Biobank and others. We also collaborate with international partners at UC San Diego and the J. Craig Venter Institute, and with leading industry partners such as Nvidia, OpenAI, and Microsoft. The work will include development, implementation, testing, documentation, and application of reproducible analytical pipelines.

About the position We are offering a PhD Research Fellow (SKO 1017) in political science with a specialization in public policy and public administration. The successful applicant will be part of the research project &#34;How Policy Growth Affects Policy Implementation and Performance&#34; (POLIGROW) funded by the Norwegian Research Council. The PhD fellowship period is three years and devoted to carrying out research and pursuing a PhD in the context of the POLIGROW project. The position may also include other career-enhancing activities: depending on the candidate’s competence and the teaching needs of the department, the fellowship period may be extended to a maximum of four years to accommodate qualifying activities, such as teaching responsibilities. The POLIGROW project studies how policy growth affects the administrative structures, working routines and performance of the authorities in charge of implementation. The project is organized in three work packages. The first work package maps and explains how policy growth changes administrative structures, drawing on new hand-coded data on implementation tasks and the public and private organizations responsible for them in environmental/climate and social policy across 15 OECD countries from 1975 to 2025. The second work package examines how policy growth affects the working routines and coping practices of bureaucrats, such as policy triage, negative coordination, and selective oversight of private contractors, through comparative case studies, document analysis and semi-structured interviews with public officials. The third work package analyses how different patterns of burden allocation affect policy effectiveness, linking administrative structures to environmental and social performance using time-series-cross-section and difference-in-differences designs. POLIGROW will produce the first cross-national dataset covering all implementing authorities and their tasks across multiple sectors, to be published on a publicly accessible data platform. The project also places strong emphasis on societal impact, with policy briefs, workshops and webinars organized with practitioners.  The PhD Fellow is expected to develop an independent research project within the overarching thematic scope of POLIGROW. The individual project should align with the project's overall framework, for example by focusing on one of the three work packages, a specific policy sector, or a subset of countries. The candidate will have considerable freedom in developing their research questions, theoretical approach, and methodological design, whether quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods. At the same time, the PhD Fellow will play an active role in the project's collective research activities, particularly in Work Package I. This includes contributing to the development of the coding manual and the coding of implementation structures. Candidates are therefore expected to design a PhD project that makes substantial use of data generated through POLIGROW: data they help collect themselves and/or other data produced within the project. The intention is that the collaborative data collection forms an integral part of the PhD project and provides a foundation for the candidate's own research. The research project proposal should include a tentative overall thematic and theoretical framework, along with at least three ideas for academic articles (see details below). The successful candidate will be part of the Faculty’s PhD programme. The work is expected to lead to a PhD in political science. To promote a strategic career path, all PhD fellows are expected to submit a career development plan, specifying career goals and the competencies that the PhD fellow should acquire, no later than one month after commencement of the fellowship period. The department is responsible for ensuring that the plan is followed up and that the PhD fellow has access to career guidance throughout the duration of the fellowship. Qualification requirements Formal qualifications • The applicant’s prior education must correspond to five years of university-level education in Norway in political science or a related field, of which two years (120 ECTS credits) are at the master's level. • The master's degree should have a conventional academic and theoretical orientation and not be an experience-based or professionally oriented master's degree. • The master's thesis must be equivalent to at least half a year's full-time study (30 ECTS credits). • The master's degree must include relevant training in research design and methods. • Applicants must have an excellent academic record, normally corresponding to grades in the upper range of the Norwegian A – F grading scale (i.e., A (excellent) – B (very good)), or comparable academic achievement under other grading systems. • Please see the admissions requirements for the PhD programme for further details on qualification requirements. Applications from candidates who do not fulfil all formal qualification requirements will be excluded from further assessment.  Other qualifications • Applicants must have a very good to excellent command of English language (written and oral). • Language skills relevant to the countries covered by the project (e.g. German, French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese or a Scandinavian language) are an advantage.  • Applicants are required to possess robust empirical research knowledge within political science, with proficiency in quantitative or qualitative analysis. • Experience with manual data coding or interview-based research is an advantage but not a requirement. • Possessing knowledge of public administration, policy implementation, or environmental/climate or social policy is an advantage but not a requirement.

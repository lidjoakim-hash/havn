You will find more information about working at NTNU and the application process here.     Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v&#61;_KHQjc4ndas&t&#61;41sAbout the position The position is a 3-year doctoral research fellowship starting in 2027. The PhD candidate will be embedded in the welding and joining research activities of SFI FAST and will work closely with NTNU, SINTEF, and leading international industrial partners in the aluminium value chain. The PhD position focuses on understanding the influence of impurities coming from recycled Aluminium on the weldability of different alloys. These impurities can alter the weldability, microstructure, and performance of Aluminium welds compared to the reference alloy. Solidification and liquation cracking, pore formation, heat-affected zone softening, and degradation of corrosion resistance are some examples of possible challenges. However, the knowledge is limited about any of these possible changes. Therefore, the candidate needs to provide a thorough investigation of the welding process using conventional techniques and develop new methodologies. In addition, the welding procedure adoption to enhance the performance of the alloys based on their metallurgical characteristics shall be developed. Therefore, the candidate should have a great understanding of the welding process-microstructure-performance.   Your immediate leader will be the Head of Department. You will be supervised by Professor Vahid Hosseini. About the project SFI FAST (Future Aluminium Structures) is a national Centre for Research-based Innovation (SFI) funded by the Research Council of Norway and the aluminum industry. The Centre is led by NTNU in close collaboration with SINTEF and a broad consortium of 15 leading industrial partners across the aluminium value chain. FAST runs from 2026 to 2034 and represents one of Norway’s most ambitious long-term research initiatives within aluminium materials, manufacturing technologies, and circular economy. The primary objective of FAST is to establish the scientific and technological knowledge base required to enable the large-scale use of post-consumer scrap (PCS) aluminium in high-value, structural, and safety-critical products. FAST adopts a through-scale and through-process research methodology, integrating materials science, manufacturing processes, product and structural design, and lifecycle performance. A key outcome of FAST is the development of the FAST Virtual Lab, a digital framework combining experimental data, physics-based models, and data-driven methods to support design, manufacturing, and decision-making across aluminium value chains. Education and competence building are central pillars of FAST. The Centre will educate more than 18 PhD and postdoctoral researchers and over 100 MSc students, strengthening Norway’s international position in aluminium research and innovation. The two positions posted here will be among the first ones to start in the Centre.

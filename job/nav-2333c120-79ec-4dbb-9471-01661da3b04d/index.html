About the research group The Drones and Autonomous Systems research group is one of seven research groups in NORCE’s Technology and Digital Systems division, which has approximately 110 employees. Based in Tromsø, the group works nationally and internationally and has substantial activity in EU and other European-funded projects. We develop knowledge and technology in close collaboration with industry, public-sector organizations and research partners. Our work spans autonomy, remote sensing and advanced sensor systems, with a strong emphasis on solutions that address real operational needs. A significant and growing part of our portfolio consists of dual-use and security-related projects. We develop drone and autonomous-system technologies for civilian, preparedness and security applications, often in demanding maritime and Arctic environments. • Autonomous vehicles and robotic systems for aerial, terrestrial and maritime operations • Navigation, guidance and control, including operations in GNSS-denied and otherwise challenging environments • Sensors, distributed sensor networks and communication solutions • Computer vision, artificial intelligence, machine learning and advanced data analytics • Signal processing, radar technology and imaging systems • Remote sensing and integrated observation systems using drones, aircraft and satellites • Operational concepts, safety and services for advanced drone missions Our projects combine scientific excellence with practical implementation. We conduct research on autonomous transport, inspection and monitoring services; integrate drone, aircraft and satellite observations; develop smart sensors; and use advanced analytics to create solutions for climate and environmental monitoring, transport, infrastructure, preparedness and security. We are currently recruiting for the following position: Researcher / Senior Researcher – Cybernetics, Drones and Autonomous Systems You will contribute to research and innovation projects in drones and autonomous systems, working across autonomy, navigation, control, remote sensing and advanced sensor platforms. The role combines scientific work, technology development and practical implementation in close collaboration with industry, public-sector organizations and academic partners. Developing and securing new projects is an important part of the role. We are particularly interested in candidates with strong mathematical and algorithmic skills and experience in real-time processing, navigation, control or perception. Practical experience with drones, robotics, embedded systems or sensor integration is highly relevant. Knowledge of research and innovation funding schemes and previous experience in developing research projects will be an advantage. The position is based in Tromsø. Competence areas • Autonomous navigation, guidance and control for robotic and sensor systems • IMU/INS, sensor fusion, localisation and time synchronisation • Real-time sensor processing and algorithm development • Computer vision and radar-, LiDAR- or camera-based perception • ArduPilot, PX4 or comparable autopilot software • Optical, multispectral, thermal and radar imaging • Simulation of complex systems in Gazebo, AirSim or similar environments • Hardware integration, interfaces and practical engineering Required qualifications • A PhD in a relevant field • Experience in project management or coordination • Documented experience developing tenders, research proposals or securing externally funded projects • Experience using AI tools in professional research and development workflows • Strong written and verbal communication skills in English and Norwegian • The ability to work independently and systematically while contributing effectively in interdisciplinary teams • The ability to manage several activities and projects in parallel Applicants with a documented professional network in the drone industry and/or academia, together with a strong track record in developing successful research and innovation projects, will be prioritized. Experience from dual-use, security, preparedness or defense-related projects will be considered an advantage.

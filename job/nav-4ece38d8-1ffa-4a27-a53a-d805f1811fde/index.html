About the position Starting date as soon as possible. The appointment is a full-time position and is for a period 6 months. Place of work is Department of Geosciences, Section for Meteorology and Oceanography at Blindern, Oslo Norway. The position is associated with the NORCLI (Nordic Climate History) project. NORCLI is an interdisciplinary project led by Prof. Dominik Collet at the Department of Archaeology, Conservation and History and co-lead by Professor Kirstin Krüger at the Department of Geosciences, Section for Meteorology and Oceanography, at the University of Oslo. It is financed by the University of Oslo Thematic Research Group. NORCLI is investigating Nordic Climate History: What happened the last time Nordic societies encountered rapid climate change? How did natural and social environments interact during the Little Ice Age (1300-1900), also in response to major volcanic events (i.e., 1690s, 1783-1783 Laki)? What can be learned from the ways people adapted to challenges that are in many ways similar to the ones we face today? The Nordic Climate History (NORCLI) research renders these fundamental questions researchable, using a novel 'socionatural' approach that integrates climatology and history and connects them to museology. NORCLI is working in three steps: (1) reconnecting available data from both the archives of nature (lake sediments, tree-rings, ice cores), climate physics (climate model data) and of society (historical records, material culture) with a focus on extreme events initiated by abrupt climate change in the last two millennia; (2) integrating interactions ‘from agriculture to culture’ bringing natural scientists and historians together; and (3) narrating results to a wider museum and societal audience. The Quaternary Geology and Paleoclimate Researcher is expected to contribute to the working steps (1) and (2). The researcher shall be an experienced independent scientist within quaternary geology and paleoclimate specialized on varved lake sediments and their analyses in the Nordic countries. In addition, expertise on floods, hydroclimate variations, and natural hazards in particular in Norway during the Holocene are expected as well. As the candidate will be working closely with the other NORCLI positions on history, ice cores, tree rings, and climate modelling, we are seeking for a team player with an interdisciplinary background. Previous documented experience with the analyses of varved lake sediments in Norway next to other paleo climate proxies, and the historical archives on climate is required.

Department of Forest Ecology and Management   Scientific background Plants do more with organic nitrogen than use it for growth. Amino acids and their derivatives can also be diverted into signalling pathways that shape development and help plants respond to environmental stress. We are seeking a motivated Postdoctoral Researcher to investigate how this redirection of organic nitrogen metabolism is regulated and what it means for plant stress tolerance. About the position   The project explores the links between organic nitrogen metabolism, metabolic regulation and downstream signalling cascades, and their roles in plant responses to abiotic and biotic challenges, including pathogen attack. The work will combine physiological, biochemical, molecular and advanced imaging approaches, using Arabidopsis thaliana alongside crop species in sterile, hydroponic and soil-based systems. The position is particularly suitable for a candidate who wishes to build an independent research profile at the interface of plant nutrition, metabolic signalling and stress biology Key responsibilities The successful candidate will: Plan and carry out experiments on plant growth, development and stress responses under controlled conditions Use chemical and genetic approaches to manipulate organic nitrogen metabolism and signalling Perform gene expression, metabolite and biochemical analyses Carry out microscopy-based analyses, including confocal and two-photon imaging Develop and run stress and infection mimicking bioassays as well as nematode assays Analyse and interpret data, and publish results in international scientific journals Contribute to experimental planning and take an active part in the group’s national and international collaborations, and in the supervision of students Your profile Required qualifications PhD in plant biology, plant physiology, molecular biology, biochemistry or a closely related discipline.  Documented experience with experimental plant research, experimental design and data analysis Documented scientific publishing in international peer-reviewed journals Ability to plan, execute and document experiments independently Very good written and spoken English Meritorious qualifications   Experience in one or more of the following areas will be considered a strong advantage: Gene expression analysis, particularly qRT-PCR In vitro sterile culture techniques Work with Arabidopsis thaliana and crop species Two-photon microscopy and advanced confocal microscopy Hydroponic cultivation and soil-based treatments Mass spectrometry (MS) and metabolite analysis Phytopathology and plant–pathogen interactions Bioassay development and implementation, particularly nematode culture and infection assays Great importance will be placed on personal qualities such as curiosity, independence, problem-solving ability and the ability to work well with others. As postdoctoral appointments are career-developing positions for junior researchers, we are primarily looking for candidates with a doctoral degree that is three years old at most. If there are special circumstances, such a degree may have been awarded earlier. Special circumstances include leave due to illness, parental leave, positions of trust within trade union organizations, service within the total defence system, or other similar circumstances, as well as clinical service or other employment/assignments relevant to the subject area.   About us   The position is based at the Department of Forest Ecology and Management at SLU in Umeå, within the research group led by Professor Torgny Näsholm. The group investigates plant nitrogen nutrition, with a particular focus on the acquisition and utilization of organic nitrogen and their roles in plant physiology and growth. The project will be supervised day to day by Dr Ioanna Antoniadi-Nilsson. The Postdoc. will be employed at The Department of Forest Ecology and Management. The department includes about 120 people of which 9 are professors.

Department of Applied Animal Science and Welfare Third-cycle subject area Animal Science Description of the doctoral project This PhD project aims to establish subjective time perception as a new animal-centred dimension of animal welfare. It combines animal welfare science, ethology, comparative cognition, affective neuroscience and human time-perception research. The project will examine how affective state, predictability and agency influence animals’ temporal experience, using dogs as the principal model species. About the position The PhD student will: conduct a structured interdisciplinary review of time perception and its relevance to animal welfare adapt, pilot and validate an interval-timing task for dogs design and conduct experiments examining timing behaviour under neutral, positive and mildly negative affective conditions train dogs using positive reinforcement and collect behavioural and physiological data analyse video recordings, operant responses, heart rate, heart-rate variability and salivary cortisol contribute to the development of a conceptual framework for studying time perception across companion, farm, laboratory and zoo animals publish the findings in international scientific journals and present them at national and international conferences You will join an interdisciplinary and international research environment with expertise in animal welfare, animal cognition, and affective states. The position provides opportunities to develop skills in animal experimentation, cognitive testing, behavioural analysis, physiological measurements, statistics and scientific communication. Requirements To meet the general entry requirements you must have been awarded a second-cycle (Master’s) qualification, satisfied the requirements for courses comprising at least 240 credits of which at least 60 credits were awarded in the second-cycle, or acquired substantially equivalent knowledge in some other way in Sweden or abroad (Higher Education Ordinance 1993:100, Chapter 7, Section 39). The specific entry requirements for the subject animal science are knowledge corresponding to at least 120 higher education credits in biology subjects, of which a total of at least 30 credits must be in animal science. This must include at least 30 credits at advanced level and the student must have demonstrated the ability to work independently in a degree project. Applicants with a different, comparable educational background may be eligible; this will be decided by the faculty board on a case-by case basis. You must also meet the equivalent English language requirements as those necessary for the Swedish upper-secondary qualification Engelska B/6. Your profile At the time of employment, you must have completed a Master’s degree, or equivalent, in animal science, biology, ethology, veterinary science, psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience or another relevant subject. You should have: a strong interest in animal behaviour, animal welfare and cognition experience or training in experimental research and quantitative data analysis experience in ethogram development, behavioural observations and/or video coding the ability to plan, document and complete research tasks independently excellent written and spoken English Experience considered a merit: handling or training dogs or other animals using positive reinforcement cognitive or operant testing collection or analysis of heart-rate variability, cortisol or other physiological data statistical analysis in R or similar software As a person, you should be curious, patient, analytical and attentive to detail. You should be comfortable working both independently and collaboratively and have a responsible and ethically sensitive approach to research involving animals. Good organisational and communication skills are important because the project involves collaboration across scientific disciplines and with international research partners.

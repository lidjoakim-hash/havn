7Department of Forest Mycology and Plant Pathology Join the Department of Forest Mycology and Plant Pathology at the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU) for a two-year postdoctoral position using population genomics to discover how rye adapts to different environments and what role its microbiota play. You will lead the genomic work in the Swedish Research Council-funded project ‘Eco-evolution of rye and its microbial allies’, and also carry out some field work and greenhouse studies. About the position Rye (Secale cereale L.) is cultivated where other cereals struggle, tolerating cold, drought and poor soils. Unlike its close relatives wheat and barley, rye is outcrossing, elabling it to maintain high genetic diversity. This makes rye a powerful system for understanding whether microbes help shape how plants adapt to their environment and how domestication has disrupted this. Landrace rye is also making a comeback on farms, so we can study living populations in the places that shaped them, rather than relying only on gene bank samples. You will study rye populations ranging from wild and weedy relatives to landraces and inbred elite lines. Your main tasks will be to: collect seeds from rye populations across northern Europe, grow them under standardised conditions in the greenhouse, and characterise their physiological and phenological traits and associated microbial communities analyse whole-genome sequencing data to describe the genetic diversity and population structure of the rye populations link genomic variation to plant traits, microbial communities and the environment to identify drivers of adaptation, for example through genome–environment association analyses and selection scans You will work within the Soil Microbiology Group at the Department of Forest Mycology and Plant Pathology, whose focus is on understanding the role microorganisms play in biogeochemical processes. You will also be connected to a wider group of collaborators who support the genomic work, including evolutionary biologists at Uppsala University, Sweden, and crop genomic specialists at the University of Melbourne, Australia. Your profile We are looking for a person with a PhD in biology with a focus on population genetics, evolutionary biology, bioinformatics, ecology, agricultural science, or a related field, with documented capabilities of autonomous scientific research work and skills in writing scientific publications. The applicant should also be highly motivated, open-minded and team-oriented, and contribute positively to the development of the research environment. Qualifications: experience of analysing high-throughput sequencing data for population genomics, from raw reads to population-level analysis experience of working from the command line on a computing cluster and of scripting, for example in bash, R or Pythongood good written and spoken English, sufficient for scientific writing, reporting and participation in international project activities It is an advantage if you have: experience of whole-genome sequencing data, especially from large genomes experience of associating genomic variation with traits or the environment knowledge of evolutionary and eco-evolutionary processes in plants experience of working with plants or microbial community data a driver’s licence We do not expect you to have all of these advantages. If your genomics experience comes from another study system, you are very welcome to apply, and we will support you in learning the plant and microbial work. A position as a postdoctoral researcher is intended for a researcher early in their career. We are therefore primarily looking for candidates who completed their doctoral degree no more than three years before the application deadline. If your degree is older because of parental leave, illness, union duties, military service or similar circumstances, please state this in your application.

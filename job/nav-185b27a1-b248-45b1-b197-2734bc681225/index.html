About the position We invite applications for a Researcher position in computational chemistry, available at the Hylleraas centre, Department of Chemistry. The position is for a period of 2 years. Starting date no later than 01.03.2027. Place of work is Hylleraas centre, Department of Chemistry at Blindern, Oslo. Project description The position is funded by the Research Council of Norway through the Researcher Project for Early Career Scientists (FRIPRO) “APExModel: Addressing Antimicrobial Peptides by Integrating Experiments into Modelling” and lead by Henrique Musseli Cezar.  Antimicrobial resistance is a critical global health crisis, and new drugs are urgently needed.  APExModel will establish a set of experimentally-grounded design principles that can aid the development of safer and more effective AMPs. This will be done by developing and applying a new computational framework that integrates experimental data into simulations. The project will also provide: 1) better understanding the molecular mechanisms that govern how AMPs self-assemble in solution, a key factor for their stability and activity; 2) a novel simulation methodology that can accurately model AMPs interacting with cell membranes by using experimental small-angle scattering data to guide the simulations; and 3) insights on the action mechanisms of different AMPs and how they disrupt bacterial membranes. The most critical R&D challenge is the development of this new simulation methodology. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations provide access to dynamics at atomic resolution, but limitations in simulation timescale and accuracy may compromise the agreement with experiments. Current integrative approaches are limited to systems with spherical symmetry and cannot adequately model the flat, patch-like membrane used in most MD simulations of AMP-membrane studies. This project will overcome this limitation, adding a capability that is currently missing in the field. Combining expertise from chemistry, pharmacy, and physics, this interdisciplinary project will produce a new open-source simulation tool and the fundamental knowledge required to establish design principles for AMPs.

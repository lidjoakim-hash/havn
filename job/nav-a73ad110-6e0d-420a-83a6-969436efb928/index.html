You can find more information about working at NTNU and the application process here.     Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v&#61;WWHqR5i1FHE&feature&#61;youtu.beAbout the Job The Department of Information Security and Communication Technology (IIK) at NTNU Gjøvik has a vacancy for a Researcher in Socio-Technical Fraud Systems.  The position is for an intended period of 24 months and is part of SAFE – Secure Anti-Fraud Excellence Center, funded by Sparebankstiftelsen Hedmark and SpareBank 1 Østlandet in collaboration with NTNU and industry partners. The researcher will be a key member of WP1 and lead its socio-technical root cause analysis of financial fraud, hosted at CCIS, NTNU Gjøvik. WP1 examines vishing and safe-account fraud from both the attacker and the victim perspective and a proposed extension to investment fraud. It distinguishes technical authentication and payment confirmation from legally valid consent. The research reconstructs how fraud unfolds across channels, while focusing detection and intervention on evidence actually visible to the bank between customer authentication and payment authorisation. Off-bank calls, screen sharing and remote-access activities are described where case sources support them, but are not assumed to be observable by the bank. The work draws on public decisions of Finansklagenemnda, court judgments, and anonymised or otherwise lawfully accessible cases from SAFE’s banking partners. Results feed into cognitive experiments (WP2), detection research (WP3), scientific publications and a policy white paper. Center for Cyber and Information Security (CCIS) at NTNU will host the SAFE Project. Your immediate leader is head of department. Duties of the position • Build a systematically coded corpus of fraud cases from public decisions and lawfully accessible bank material, using a shared template and a cross-channel timeline. • Develop evidence-based fraud scripts for vishing and safe-account fraud and, subject to scope confirmation, investment fraud, including interim inputs for WP2. • Map each fraud stage, the channels involved, signs of real-time guidance, and what is actually observable by the bank between authentication and payment authorisation. • Identify legitimate customer activities with similar bank-visible patterns, to support realistic false-alarm testing without using victim characteristics as automatic detection features. • Study intervention points and fraudster adaptation, including cases where the bank suspects fraud but the customer rejects warnings; help plan a tabletop session with a banking partner. • Analyse legal, ethical and governance conditions for collecting, using and sharing fraud-related signals, including privacy, bank confidentiality and interbank cooperation. • Examine the conditions for transaction warnings, delays, refusals and escalation, with attention to customer consent, liability and loss allocation. • Assess payee verification and, in investment-fraud cases, checks of providers against Finanstilsynet’s register and market warnings; distinguish these from bank-account verification. • Provide fraud scripts and realistic look-alike scenarios to WP2 and observability findings, intervention points and lawful-signal constraints to WP3. • Prepare WP1 deliverables, scientific publications and a policy white paper, and present findings to the SAFE Advisory Board and project partners. • Contribute, where relevant, to SAFE continuing education (EVU), project reporting and research proposal development. Required selection criteria • A master’s degree (120 ECTS or equivalent) or a PhD in information security, socio-technical systems, criminology, information systems, law and technology, or a closely related field. • Documented experience with systematic analysis of qualitative material, such as case files, legal decisions, interviews or incident reports, using a coding scheme or structured method. • Knowledge of social engineering and fraud in digital channels, such as vishing, authorised push payment fraud or investment fraud. • Understanding of digital banking and payment processes, including authentication, payment initiation and customer interaction channels. • Ability to recognise legal, ethical and data-protection questions and work with relevant specialists on their implications for fraud prevention research. • Reading comprehension of Norwegian or another Scandinavian language sufficient to analyse Norwegian case material and legal decisions. • Good written and spoken English and the ability to prepare clear, well-structured research reports. Preferred selection criteria • A PhD in a relevant field, or research experience after completion of the master’s degree. • Experience in applied research projects involving banks, financial crime specialists or regulatory and legal experts. • Experience in crime script analysis, situational crime prevention, socio-technical analysis, or process and threat modelling. • Knowledge of Norwegian or EU rules on payment services, data protection, bank confidentiality or financial crime prevention. • Experience in fraud prevention, anti-money laundering, compliance or incident handling in the financial sector. • Experience in planning and facilitating interviews, workshops or tabletop exercises with practitioners. • Relevant scientific publications or reports on fraud, cybercrime or socio-technical security. • Skills in structuring and analysing data, for example using Python, R or NVivo. • Spoken Norwegian or another Scandinavian language for cooperation with partner banks.

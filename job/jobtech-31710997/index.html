Örebro University and the School of Science and Technology are looking for a doctoral student for the doctoral programme in Mechanical Engineering, concluding with a doctoral degree. Start date: January – March 2027. Project description Steel wire is an essential component of countless products and applications, from everyday items such as ballpoint pens and car tyres to critical infrastructure such as buildings and bridges. As industry advances towards more sustainable manufacturing, there is an increasing need to produce steel wire products with greater resource efficiency, improved material performance, and reduced environmental impact. This doctoral project aims to advance the knowledge and technologies needed to make wire drawing processes more efficient, robust, and sustainable. The research is carried out in close collaboration with the wire drawing industry, ensuring that scientific developments address real industrial challenges and contribute to more sustainable production of steel wire products. Particular emphasis will be placed on improving process understanding and developing and evaluating innovative drawing tools. The research will combine experimental investigations of wire drawing processes, tool behaviour, and resulting wire properties with sensor-based measurements, data analysis, and modelling. A central objective is to understand how material properties, process conditions, lubrication, and tooling interact and influence process performance, tool wear, and wire quality. This knowledge will support the development of practical solutions that improve resource utilisation, extend tool life, and enhance the efficiency and sustainability of industrial wire drawing. As a doctoral student, you will plan and conduct experiments, analyse data, develop models, and communicate your findings through scientific publications and presentations. You will work closely with academic researchers and industrial partners, contributing to research that bridges fundamental scientific understanding and practical industrial applications. The project offers an interdisciplinary research environment at the intersection of mechanical engineering, manufacturing technology, materials engineering, measurement technology, modelling, and control engineering. By addressing challenges associated with resource efficiency, production performance, and the green transition, the research aims to contribute to the development of next-generation wire drawing processes and tools that support more sustainable steel wire production. Supervision: The doctoral student will be supervised by Christer Korin (principal supervisor) and Joakim Larsson (assistant supervisor). The programme and the doctoral studentship The doctoral programme consists of courses and an independent research project that you will present in a doctoral thesis. The programme concludes with a doctoral degree and comprises 240 credits, which corresponds to four years of full-time study. Our ambition is for your doctoral studies to be stimulating and purposeful throughout the programme until you have obtained your doctoral degree. A thorough introduction will therefore get you off to a good start and provide a solid foundation on which you can build your studies. As a doctoral student at Örebro University, you will be offered a specially tailored seminar series, covering matters ranging from doctoral programme rules and careers to support during the study period and networking. The place on the programme is linked to a full-time doctoral studentship for the duration of the study programme, which corresponds to four years of full-time study. More information on doctoral studentships, part-time studies and part-time doctoral studentships can be found on https://www.oru.se/english/study/doctoral-studies/how-to-become-a-doctoral-student/.

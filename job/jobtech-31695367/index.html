Are you looking for a meaningful specialist position where you can combine professional development with a high quality of life? This is an opportunity to work within child and adolescent psychiatry in a close-knit Nordic healthcare environment, while living in a peaceful island community surrounded by nature and the sea. About the Island This Nordic island is home to approximately 30,000 residents and offers a safe, peaceful and family-friendly environment. With its beautiful archipelago, clean air and easy access to outdoor activities such as hiking, sailing and cycling, the island provides excellent opportunities for an active lifestyle outside of work. For professionals considering relocation with their families, the island offers schools, daycare services and a welcoming community within a compact geographical area. About the psychiatric service The child and adolescent psychiatric service covers the entire island and serves approximately 5,900 children and adolescents aged 0–17. The service is primarily outpatient-based and currently has around 140 active patients, with approximately 210 referrals per year. About 50% of referrals relate to neurodevelopmental conditions, making ADHD and autism an important part of the clinical work. Common psychiatric conditions include: Depression Anxiety disorders Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) ADHD Autism Other child and adolescent psychiatric conditions Treatment includes psychological and psychotherapeutic interventions, pharmacological treatment, psychoeducation and support for both patients and their families. When needed, the team also collaborates with schools, social services and other healthcare providers. About the Role As a specialist in child and adolescent psychiatry, you will have a broad and varied clinical role with the opportunity to follow patients throughout their assessment and treatment. Your responsibilities will include: Assessing, diagnosing and treating children and adolescents with a broad range of psychiatric conditions Working with both general psychiatric and neurodevelopmental conditions, including ADHD and autism Developing and following up individualized treatment plans Providing pharmacological treatment when appropriate Working closely with psychologists, nurses, social workers and other healthcare professionals Collaborating with families, schools, social services and other healthcare providers when needed Participating in clinical discussions and contributing your specialist expertise to the multidisciplinary team Supporting and mentoring residents and junior doctors Contributing to the continued development of the psychiatric service Physicians typically see approximately 4–5 patients per day, alongside clinical rounds, acute assessments and other outpatient responsibilities. There may also be opportunities for further professional development and increased responsibility within the organization. Outpatient Work and Acute Psychiatry The work is predominantly outpatient-based. The child and adolescent psychiatric service does not have its own inpatient ward or separate on-call service. Acute cases are primarily handled through a reserved acute appointment at the outpatient clinic each week. Other urgent cases may present through the emergency department or psychiatric inpatient services. When required, the initial assessment can be carried out by the on-call physician within adult psychiatry, in consultation with the child and adolescent psychiatric team.

Role Description As a Senior Infrastructure Software Engineer on Dropbox’s CI Automation team, you will build the platform that helps engineers and coding agents turn code changes into trustworthy software. Our CI systems run hundreds of thousands of builds and hundreds of millions of tests each day across Dropbox products and services. You will own a core part of the platform from strategy and design through implementation, rollout, measurement, and operation. The work spans distributed scheduling and execution, caching, test intelligence, workload isolation, observability, reliability, and cost optimization. You will collaborate with teams across Dropbox to make feedback faster and more actionable while treating quality, security, provenance, and safety as core design requirements. This role is well suited to an engineer who enjoys deep systems work and close partnership with developer customers. Your work will directly influence how quickly and confidently Dropbox can build, validate, and ship software in an agentic SDLC. Join us in redefining the future, where your passion for building scalable, reliable systems will drive meaningful change on a global scale. Our Engineering Career Framework is viewable by anyone outside the company and describes what’s expected for our engineers at each of our career levels. Check out our blog post on this topic and more here. Responsibilities • Design, build, and operate distributed CI infrastructure supporting large-scale build and test workloads. • Own a core platform capability from an ambiguous developer need through measurable production impact. • Improve feedback latency, reliability, debuggability, and infrastructure efficiency across developer and agent-driven workflows. • Make data-informed architecture and prioritization decisions that balance performance, scalability, security, quality, and cost. • Drive technical alignment and adoption across developer productivity, product, platform, and infrastructure teams. • Raise the engineering bar through design reviews, operational learning, documentation, and mentorship. On-call work may be necessary occasionally to help address bugs, outages, or other operational issues, with the goal of maintaining a stable and high-quality experience for our customers.

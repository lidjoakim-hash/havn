You will find more information about working at NTNU and the application process here.    Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v&#61;_KHQjc4ndas&t&#61;41sAbout the position We are offering a three-year PhD position within the EU-funded FLEXI-BUILD project to develop and validate human-in-the-loop predictive control for energy flexibility in smart buildings. You will investigate how forecasts, building thermal dynamics and user preferences can support load shifting and peak-demand reduction while preserving indoor environmental quality and operational reliability. The position is based at the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering (IBM) at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) in Trondheim. Your research will connect building-performance modelling, data analysis and control with the practical integration of smart services into existing building management systems. The main research question is how much flexibility can be delivered reliably under real operating conditions, while maintaining indoor environmental quality and allowing users and operators to adjust or override automated actions. You will work with an international team of researchers and project partners in building energy systems, automation, digital interoperability, energy services, and social sciences and humanities (SSH). The Norwegian pilot at the ZEB Laboratory will support research using high-resolution monitoring data, simulation and targeted control experiments.  As a PhD candidate, you will work towards a doctorate while gaining experience relevant to research, higher education and professional practice in the built environment. Your immediate leader will be the Head of Department. About the project The FLEXI-BUILD project brings together 13 organizations and validates its solutions in four pilots in Greece, Norway, Belgium and Serbia, covering residential, office, educational and industrial buildings. FLEXI-BUILD develops and validates a common, open and vendor-independent architecture that upgrades existing home, building and energy management systems. It connects monitoring, forecasting, optimization and demand-response services through interoperable data models and interfaces. Technical validation is combined with a societal-readiness approach addressing comfort, transparency, trust and user control. Human-in-the-loop operation allows users and operators to influence automated decisions and provide feedback. The pilots will evaluate integration with the existing building management system, predictive and flexibility-oriented control, use of building thermal mass, and improved PV self-consumption. The PhD will make a defined research contribution to these activities by developing the interoperability framework to improve buildings’ performance, with the detailed questions and methods developed with the supervisory team within the Building Technology research group. Duties of the position • Complete doctoral education and carry out research of good scientific quality on smart-building control and energy flexibility within the framework described above • Contribute to requirements and interfaces for interoperable building systems • Develop and test control and flexibility methods at the ZEB Laboratory with project partners • Evaluate energy performance, flexibility and indoor comfort • Incorporate user needs and feedback while protecting data privacy • Academic publications and popular science dissemination and contribute to project reports and activities • Participate in the Building Technology research group • Participate in national and international activities such as conferences, consortium meetings and workshops Be prepared for changes to your work duties after employment.

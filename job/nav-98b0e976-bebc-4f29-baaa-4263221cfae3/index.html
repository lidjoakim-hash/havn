About the position At the Centre for Precision Psychiatry, Institute of Clinical Medicine, University of Oslo, we have two full-time 3-year Postdoctoral Research Fellowships available within Novo Nordisk Foundation (NNF) funded projects focused on uncovering mechanistic links between mid-life metabolic trajectories and late onset Alzheimer’s disease (AD). These positions offer an exciting opportunity for highly motivated researchers to work at the interface of multi-omics, brain and body imaging, and artificial intelligence, contributing to a large-scale Norwegian, UK, and international biobank collaborations aimed at understanding how midlife metabolic dysfunction shifts the risk and timing of AD. These NNF funded projects collectively aim to uncover the molecular mechanisms linking midlife metabolic trajectories to late-onset AD, and to translate these insights into a novel explainable AI-driven multimodal framework for personalized prediction. The project integrates multi-omics data (genetics, proteomics, metabolomics), brain and body MRI, cognitive assessments, and health registry-linked data from individuals across Norway and the UK. The Centre for Precision Psychiatry has access to a rich and unique data infrastructure, including large databases of biomarkers, clinical and cognitive variables, genotyping and sequencing data, and MRI brain imaging data from patients with neuropsychiatric and severe mental disorders. In addition, the Centre actively collaborates with major international consortia, including the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium (PGC), the ENIGMA consortium, and the European Alzheimer’s Disease DNA BioBank (EADB). The research environment further benefits from access to data from a broad range of national and international studies, including Norwegian population cohorts (e.g., DemGene, HUSK, HUNT, Tromsø study, and MoBa), UK-based cohorts (EPIC-Norfolk, Fenland, and the UK Biobank), the Estonian Biobank, and the ABCD and ADNI studies. The positions are primarily focused on the development and application of statistical and machine learning methods for integrating large-scale longitudinal multi-omics data (proteomics and metabolomics), with clinical and imaging data, and genetics from Norwegian and international biobanks. The postdoctoral fellows will contribute to the following activities: (1) identifying midlife metabolic trajectories that precede neurodegeneration and AD, including metabolic subtyping; (2) linking these trajectories to established neurodegeneration markers, brain and body MRI, and cognition; (3) characterizing gene x environment interactions that shape AD risk and timing; and (4) developing and validating the “AD Avatar” for predicting age of onset. The successful candidates will work closely with an interdisciplinary research environment including statisticians, geneticists, clinicians, and data scientists, and will conduct research using state-of-the-art secure infrastructure, including the TSD platform at the University of Oslo. These postdoctoral fellowships are intended to strengthen the candidate’s scientific independence and publication record, and to provide strong qualifications for future academic or research leadership positions within precision medicine, population health, and data-driven neuroscience.
